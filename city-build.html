How the City Is Built

The living city is not modeled — it is grown from a one-line recipe. A seed and a handful of numbers become a road network, that network encloses blocks, those blocks are cut into lots, and every lot asks an architect what belongs there. The whole town is deterministic: the same recipe rebuilds the same city, every time, on any machine.

The pipeline, end to end

Each stage is pure geometry — no rendering, no physics — so the entire city is unit-tested headless (ADR-0038).

1 · The recipe

A generated road is one JSON block — a "generate" recipe (ADR-0056). The living city's is barely a dozen numbers:

radius 170 arterials 3 block_size 82 curviness 0.22 artery_width 13 street_width 7 seed 5

That is the entire authored input for the streets. Everything below is derived. Because the recipe — not the baked geometry — is what's saved, the city stays editable: widen a road, drag a node, or press the Rebuild road graph button in the debug panel and the whole town regrows from a fresh seed, roads and buildings together.

2 · Growing the road network

The recipe builds a district graph, which is then cleaned into a single connected, well-formed street network. Each pass exists because its absence produced a visible defect:

Planarize + degree cap

Every crossing becomes a shared node; no junction carries more than four arms. Capping runs last — planarizing can lift a node past the cap when a street tees into it.

Merge short edges

Crossings that land within ~14 m fold into one junction; a stub too short to merge is stretched to a drivable length. No more two-metre "roads" between intersections.

Warp into curves

A domain-warp bends the rigid grid into organic sweeping streets (curviness controls how much), so the town reads as grown rather than graph-paper.

Open acute junctions, then prune razors

A relaxation rotates arms apart so tight corners stay weldable; any pair still tighter than ~20°, which relaxation can't fix, loses its shorter arm — a clean cul-de-sac beats a razor-thin sidewalk crotch.

Kill hairpins

A through-corner sharper than ~52° would fold the stroked carriageway over itself; those are relaxed toward their chord until drivable.

The cleaned graph is then stroked into a real carriageway — an in-house polygon-join engine unions the road ribbons, rounds the junction corners, and lays curbs, sidewalks, lane markings, and crosswalks (ADR-0056). Sidewalk bands miter at corners by turn direction, so nothing z-fights or leaves a gap.

3 · Blocks and lots

The enclosed faces of the road graph are the city blocks. The town rim has no enclosed faces, so rectangular edge blocks are synthesized on the open side of boundary roads — otherwise the outskirts would stay bare. Each block interior (inset by road half-width + sidewalk) is then parcelled into lots: a recursive subdivision that yields a mix of frontages, from deep suburban plots to tight downtown slivers and the occasional flatiron wedge.

4 · The architect

Placement is not random. A radial district map tags every point of the city, and each lot asks the architect what belongs there:

Financial

The core. Glass towers and office slabs, their height peaking on a coreness falloff so the skyline is a cluster, not one lonely spike.

Commercial

Mixed-use shopfronts, brick retail, mid-rise offices, hotels — packed frontages with retail ground floors.

Residential

Yard houses, duplexes, terraced rowhouses, walk-up apartments — set back on their lots with lawns.

Old Town

A seeded historic quarter: small dense parcels, masonry, the tightest frontages in the city.

Industrial

Metal sheds and industrial offices with roller bay doors, on the periphery.

Landmarks

Placed by quota, not chance: one courthouse on the best central lot, a capitol with a dome, schools and a hospital in the residential ring, plus police, fire, market, and a university — each on a lot that fits it.

The result is a coherent town: a dense financial core fading through commercial midtown into leafy residential edges, with civic landmarks standing where a planner would actually put them.

5 · Sitting the city on terrain

The city drapes onto real CDLOD terrain (ADR-0044). Ordering matters: the city is grown before the ground is meshed, so it can hand back cut/fill footprints the terrain is then built around. Roads carve a graded corridor that follows the hills at a walkable grade; each building stamps a flat pad at its entrance-side grade, so the ground meets the walls level and the front door meets the sidewalk it faces. Coarse terrain tiles sample those footprints dilated by half a cell, so a narrow road can't slip between samples and let the hill poke through the asphalt.

See it in layers. Open the grown city and press ` for the debug panel. The Render layers section toggles Roads, Buildings, and Simulation independently — turn them off to watch the bare terrain, the graded road corridors, and the building pads underneath.
