## Methods named in the code

Published techniques we implemented. These are courtesy credits, not licence obligations:
- ACES filmic fit (Stephen Hill)
- AgX minimal fit (Benjamin Wrensch, after Troy Sobotka)
- sky and atmosphere models (Hillaire; Bruneton)
- interleaved gradient noise (Jimenez)
- PCG hash (Jarzynski & Olano)
- multiple-scattering octaves for clouds (Wrenninge)
- volumetric clouds from a weather map, cloud types and eroded noise (Schneider, Guerrilla Games: "Nubis", SIGGRAPH 2015 and 2017)
- split-sum prefiltered environment lighting (Karis 2013)
- irradiance as spherical harmonics (Ramamoorthi & Hanrahan 2001)
- a rough reflection's dominant direction (Lagarde & de Rousiers, "Moving Frostbite to PBR", 2014)
- stable fluids: semi-Lagrangian advection and a pressure projection (Stam 1999)
- Möller-Trumbore ray-triangle intersection
- SplitMix
- Chaikin corner cutting
- SAE J1100 / J826 occupant and vehicle dimensions
- easing toward a speed and off the car ahead: the Intelligent Driver Model (Treiber, Hennecke & Helbing 2000), in `src/apps/driving/act.h`
- the "could still stop in time" speed limit: the Krauss safe speed (Krauss 1998), in `src/apps/driving/act.h`
- the gap a driver gives way by, measured as post-encroachment time (Allen, Shin & Cooper 1978, "Analysis of traffic conflicts and collisions"), in `src/apps/driving/give_way.h` and the lab rule set's `gap_hard`
- finding the loops of "goes after" among cars: strongly connected components (Tarjan 1972, "Depth-first search and linear graph algorithms", SIAM Journal on Computing), and putting the cars of one in an order that keeps the fixed arrows: topological sorting (Kahn 1962, "Topological sorting of large networks", Communications of the ACM), in `src/apps/driving/conflict.h` (issue #263; cited from memory, not checked against the papers)
