Trains

An island-wide main line, a light-rail loop in each big city, stations you can walk onto, carriages you can walk through, and people who take the train to the next town. The idea that makes it cheap: a track is a road class. The road builder lays it like any lane, and the trains run on exactly the rails it drew.

The problem

Glenn (2026-10-10): "it makes sense if it follows the highway in a loop and there's a station. There should be trains going in each direction ... the train system should piggy back off our other algorithms and systems of construction -- it should define rail lines and we should probably build them like we build lanes of the freeway ... run 24 hours a day at an interval ... carriages ... like the bus where you can move around the carriages and between them and they shouldn't be jittery."

That is four systems: track that climbs and bridges like a road and crosses roads without anyone driving onto it; train motion that respects curves, platforms and the train ahead; carriages that bend round curves and carry a walking player without jitter; and riders, so stations are used. Plus everything that makes a station a place: a way in from the street, stairs, canopies, lamps, name boards, sounds.

The pipeline

From plan to passengers. The builder (blue, top) is the same lanes builder that paves the roads; the sim (blue, middle) knows nothing about how the track was made, only the railhead lines and platforms it was handed.

Track is a road class

ADR-0055 had predicted it: a new world system should arrive as "fields, rules and crossing templates, never a new pipeline stage", and named rail as "a class + level-crossing template". ADR-0153 did exactly that. RoadClassSpec grew a track flag, and the built-in classes include two tracks:

Everything the builder does for a road happens to a track: its profile is grade-limited over its window (2.5 % over 400 m for the main line, so it rides long and level), it bridges valleys and gets piers, the ground is conformed to it. Only the mesher differs: a deck whose lanes are all track is a ballast bed, not asphalt, and each track lane gets sleepers every 0.65 m and two rails at standard gauge, with no paint and no junction boxes.

    const Lane& l = r.lanes.lanes[static_cast<size_t>(li)]; const DeckHeight& H = *r.heights;
    if (l.s.size() < 2 || l.s.back() < 1.0) return;

    for (double st = 0.3; st < len; st += 0.65) {

    }

Two rules keep cars off it. Tracks are left out of the road twin, so no car, walker, lot or sign ever routes onto one. And since ADR-0159 the published deck field knows what runs on each spine (UnionSpine::use: Road, Track or Footway); a street car under a viaduct no longer took the rail's height (Glenn: "cars weirdly stuck in the tracks"). Lots keep 4.5 m off a railhead and 10.5 m beside a stop (lanesTrackKeepOut), and every tree placer asks DrawnRoad::blocksTree, which includes the trains' room: 1.75 m each side of the track plus 1 m, 5.5 m over the rails (ADR-0219, after trees grew through passing trains).

Crossings: a track is never met at grade

There are no level crossings. Any crossing with a track is a grade separation whatever the scene's rules, and the builder lifts the track over the road at its own grade unless the road already clears it. Lifting the higher of the two had raised Saltwood's streets over the line at 18 % (ADR-0154). The design stage's crossing policy says the same thing in one line (planCrossings: "a track and anything: a bridge").

separates / anyTrack: the two crossings that must be structure src/engine/procgen/city/roads/lanes/vertical_profile.h

Two more vertical rules came from the light rail. A city loop crosses a side street every ~125 m, each lift with a ~200 m approach, so no joint between track sections can be clear of all of them. So a track's holds carry across joints (chainTrackFloors): each section takes its neighbours' holds into its own stations, and the next section no longer meets a lift 4 m lower. And a road that ends under a track is structure too: a dead end under the light rail had held the line 7.3 m off its profile (ADR-0158).

What the builder hands on: RailTrack

Each track lane leaves the builder as a RailTrack: the railhead line in the direction trains run on it, about 2 m apart, and its platforms in its own stations. It rides in the city bundle as JSON (city/e<n>/rail/tracks) beside the meshes, so a train stands on exactly the rails that were drawn. A graph with a track class also keys kRailBuildTag, so the track kit can change without re-baking road-only levels.

Stations: a way in, stairs, canopies, lights

Two kinds of station (schematic). The island loop runs near ground level, so its way in climbs onto a footbridge and stairs lead down (ADR-0155). A city loop is lifted over every street it crosses, so its stations are reached from below (ADR-0158).

A station's way in is just another class: footway, a 3 m path paved as sidewalk, never painted, left out of the twin. Its grade may run to 60 %, and wherever it passes 15 % (kFootwayStairGrade) the deck is built as treads with ~0.175 m risers. The first island bake held footways to a ramp's grade: too short for 11–24 m climbs, they floated whole and dragged 26 lifts and a 13 m node mismatch through the nearby diamonds. At a stair's pitch they follow the ground and climb only the last 20–30 m. Where a footway crosses a track over a platform, stationStairs lays a flight down onto each platform along its back edge. Each station's way in also becomes a bus anchor, so a bus line calls at every station's door (ADR-0155).

ADR-0159 gave stations depth and light: ribbed steel canopies on posts every 12 m with a fascia and a linear lamp every 6 m, lamps that glow on the dusk ramp and join the street lamps' nearest-N point-light pool (so a station never takes more light slots), white-on-blue name boards 16 m from each platform end (ADR-0156), and a platform board in the HUD: every line calling here, the next two trains, and minutes until they stand at this platform.

Laying out a line

Lines are derived, not drawn, and added to a graph already written, so the island is not re-grown for them (city_plan island-rail GRAPH LEVEL --write and city-rail, world/island_rail.h).

  • The island loop (ADR-0154): the freeway's outer closed chain, resampled at 10 m and offset 45 m outward. On island 8 that clears every seaward ramp by 26 m, never touches water and crosses 10 roads. The freeway's water floors carry over, so the track bridges each river where the freeway does. A station per town hub: the nearest point of the line where a 140 m platform stands on a straight (radius ≥ 900 m), at least 150 m from any road crossing. The loop is cut between stations into edges, since a closed edge cannot close on itself. 37.0 km of track, 11.1 km of it on structure.
  • The island's rail class overrides the built-in: 3.5 % with balance 0.5 (cut as well as fill). The built-in 2.5 % fill-only class rode 28 of 37 km of coastal hills on viaducts.
  • City light rail (ADR-0157/0158): round each financial hub, a loop along its arterials and collectors through the main-line station and the most built-up street node in each of eight sectors 350–950 m out; corners cut, filleted and relaxed; set in beside the street by the graph's own widest arterial plus platforms and flights; stations on straights 550–900 m apart where the streets are densest.

RailSim: lines, a timetable, and motion

RailSim (src/apps/citysim/rail_sim.h) reads only the RailTracks. Its data is small:

TrainKind, RailLine and Train src/apps/citysim/rail_sim.h
struct TrainKind {
    std::string name = "intercity";
    double locoLength = 19.0;     // the locomotive (a modern electric: no steam)
    double carLength = 24.0;      // each passenger carriage
    int cars = 3;                 // carriages behind the locomotive
    double gap = 0.9;             // coupler to coupler, where the gangway bridges
    double width = 2.9, floor = 1.1;   // body width; floor above the railhead (a platform's top)
    double vmax = 33.0;           // m/s (120 km/h) on the straight
    double accel = 0.7, decel = 0.8;   // service rates, m/s^2
    double lateral = 1.0;         // the curve speed: v^2 / r <= this
    double dwell = 30.0;          // seconds at a platform, doors open for all but the first and last few
    double length() const { return locoLength + cars * carLength + cars * gap; }
};
// ...
struct RailLine {
    std::string name;
    std::string cls;                  // its track class (rail, light_rail, metro): what stock runs it
    std::vector<engine::Vec3> pts;    // railhead centreline, running order
    std::vector<double> s;            // station of each point
    double length = 0;

    std::vector<engine::RailStop> stops;   // in this line's stations, sorted
    std::vector<double> vlim;         // per point: the speed the track allows (curves, braking ahead of them)
    // the railhead point and the running direction at station `st` (wrapped on a loop, clamped otherwise)

    double wrap(double st) const;

};

struct Train {

The rail data. A train is little more than a distance along a line; everything else is derived from it.

Lines: tracks joined end to start

A track whose end lies within 3 m of another's start, heading on the same way (dot product of the directions above 0.7) and of the same class, runs on into it. A chain that comes back to its start is a loop. A double-track loop is therefore two lines, one each way, and nothing ever switches or crosses. Lines never join across classes: a tram never runs on into the main line.

RailSim::build: which track runs on into which src/apps/citysim/rail_sim.cpp
    // which track each one runs on into: its end within joinTol of the other's start, heading on the same way
    std::vector<int> succ(static_cast<std::size_t>(n), -1), pred(static_cast<std::size_t>(n), -1);
    for (int i = 0; i < n; ++i) {
        const auto& A = tracks[static_cast<std::size_t>(i)].pts;
        if (A.size() < 2) continue;
        const Vec3 endDir = flatDir(A[A.size() - 2], A.back());
        double best = joinTol;
        for (int j = 0; j < n; ++j) {
            const auto& B = tracks[static_cast<std::size_t>(j)].pts;
            if (j == i || B.size() < 2 || tracks[static_cast<std::size_t>(j)].cls != tracks[static_cast<std::size_t>(i)].cls) continue;   // (a tram never runs on into the main line)
            const double d = dist(A.back(), B.front());
            if (d >= best) continue;
            const Vec3 startDir = flatDir(B[0], B[1]);
            if (endDir.x * startDir.x + endDir.z * startDir.z < 0.7) continue;
            best = d;
            succ[static_cast<std::size_t>(i)] = j;
        }
    }

The speed the track allows

Each point gets a limit from the curve through its neighbours five points either side (v²/r ≤ the kind's lateral, 1 m/s²), then a backward pass brakes for it ahead of time, so a train meets a curve already slow enough:

The per-point speed limit: curvature, then braked for ahead src/apps/citysim/rail_sim.cpp
        // THE SPEED THE TRACK ALLOWS: the curve's (v^2 r <= lateral, measured over 10 m either side), then braked
        // for ahead of time -- a train meets a curve already slow enough for it
        const TrainKind kind = kindOf(L.cls);
        const std::size_t m = L.pts.size();
        L.vlim.assign(m, kind.vmax);
        for (std::size_t i = 0; i < m; ++i) {
            const std::size_t span = 5;
            std::size_t a = i >= span ? i - span : (L.loop ? i + m - 1 - span : 0);
            std::size_t c = i + span < m ? i + span : (L.loop ? i + span - (m - 1) : m - 1);
            if (a >= m) a = 0;
            if (c >= m) c = m - 1;
            const double k = curvature(L.pts[a], L.pts[i], L.pts[c]);
            if (k > 1e-6) L.vlim[i] = std::min(kind.vmax, std::sqrt(kind.lateral / k));
        }
        for (int pass = 0; pass < (L.loop ? 2 : 1); ++pass)
            for (std::size_t i = m - 1; i-- > 0;) {
                const double ds = L.s[i + 1] - L.s[i];
                const double next = (i + 1 == m - 1 && L.loop) ? L.vlim[0] : L.vlim[i + 1];
                L.vlim[i] = std::min(L.vlim[i], std::sqrt(next * next + 2.0 * kind.decel * ds));
            }

The timetable is the spacing

There is no schedule. A line's trains start evenly spread round it, and since every train runs the same rules, the spacing holds. How many: an intercity per ~9 km of line, a city train per ~1.5 km, two at least. The island's 37 km loop at first ran two each way, a train every ~14 minutes, and its platforms filled with people waiting (46 at 21 minutes), so it now runs four.

countOf: trains per line src/apps/citysim/train_system.cpp

    // loop ran two each way, a train every ~14 minutes, and its platforms filled with people waiting (46 at 21 min)

        if (const char* n = std::getenv("RT_TRAINS_PER_LINE")) return std::max(1, std::atoi(n));
        return std::max(2, static_cast<int>(std::lround(length / (k.name == "intercity" ? 9000.0 : 1500.0))));
    };
The timetable: evenly spread, each heading for the first platform ahead src/apps/citysim/rail_sim.cpp

        const TrainKind kind = kindOf(L.cls);

                if (L.stops[si].s1 - 2.0 > t.s) { t.nextStop = static_cast<int>(si); break; }
            trains_.push_back(t);
        }
    }

One step

Each fixed step a running train picks a target speed, the smallest of four limits, and accelerates or brakes toward it. There are no signal blocks: the train ahead is simply a fourth limit, braking to stop 40 m short of its tail. Within reach of the platform at under 3 m/s it stops exactly there and dwells; the doors open two seconds after it stops and shut four before it leaves, on the platform's side.

One train's speed between two platforms (schematic): the line speed, a curve the train slowed for before reaching it, the braking parabola into the platform, the dwell, and away.

RailSim::step: four limits and a platform stop src/apps/citysim/rail_sim.cpp

        double toStop = 1e9;
        if (!L.stops.empty()) {
            const engine::RailStop& st = L.stops[static_cast<std::size_t>(t.nextStop)];
            toStop = st.s1 - kStopShort - t.s;
            if (L.loop) toStop = L.wrap(toStop);
            target = std::min(target, std::sqrt(2.0 * t.kind.decel * std::max(0.0, toStop)));
        }
        const double gap = gapAhead(static_cast<int>(ti));
        target = std::min(target, std::sqrt(2.0 * t.kind.decel * std::max(0.0, gap - 40.0)));
        if (t.v < target) t.v = std::min(target, t.v + t.kind.accel * dt);
        else t.v = std::max(target, t.v - 1.5 * t.kind.decel * dt);

            ds = std::max(0.0, toStop);
            t.v = 0;
            t.dwelling = true;
            t.dwellLeft = t.kind.dwell;
        }

Carriages without jitter

A carriage's pose is a function of the head's station alone. Each car's front is a fixed distance behind the head; its body hangs between two bogie points at 18 % and 82 % of its length, each sampled on the railhead line. The renderer interpolates only the head between its last two steps (headAt(alpha)), and every carriage, and anyone in it, follows exactly the curve the rails draw at that moment. That is the whole anti-jitter argument.

carPose: the body between its bogies src/apps/citysim/rail_sim.cpp
CarPose RailSim::carPose(int ti, int car, double alpha) const {
    const Train& t = trains_[static_cast<std::size_t>(ti)];
    const RailLine& L = lines_[static_cast<std::size_t>(t.line)];
    double front = headAt(ti, alpha);
    for (int k = 0; k < car; ++k) front -= (k == 0 ? t.kind.locoLength : t.kind.carLength) + t.kind.gap;
    const double len = car == 0 ? t.kind.locoLength : t.kind.carLength;
    // the body hangs between its two bogies, each on the rail
    const Vec3 p1 = L.at(front - 0.18 * len), p2 = L.at(front - 0.82 * len);
    Vec3 f = p1 - p2;
    const double fl = f.length();
    f = fl > 1e-9 ? f * (1.0 / fl) : Vec3(0, 0, 1);
    Vec3 left = engine::cross(Vec3(0, 1, 0), f);
    const double ll = left.length();
    left = ll > 1e-9 ? left * (1.0 / ll) : Vec3(1, 0, 0);
    const Vec3 up = engine::cross(f, left);
    const Vec3 c = (p1 + p2) * 0.5;

Aboard: the player as a floor point

The bus had already solved riding without jitter, and the train reuses its answer (ADR-0153). A player aboard is not a physics body pushed along by the carriage; they are a floor point in one car's frame (local_), walked by the ordinary move keys inside the aisle, vestibules and doorways (walkableAt). The eye is placed on that point of the carriage as drawn this frame, in render(), after the steps, so the view and the carriage cannot disagree. Two transitions:

ride(): through the gangway, and out of an open door src/apps/citysim/train_system.cpp
    // THROUGH THE GANGWAY: past the coupling's middle, the point belongs to the next car
    const Real hl = static_cast<Real>(coachOf(train_).length) / 2, half = static_cast<Real>(T.kind.gap) / 2;
    if (local_.z > hl + half - 0.05 || local_.z < -hl - half + 0.05) {
        const int to = local_.z > 0 ? car_ - 1 : car_ + 1;
        if (to >= 1 && to < sim_.carCount(train_)) {
            const Vec3 w = P.m.transformPoint(local_);
            const CarPose Q = sim_.carPose(train_, to, 1.0);
            Vec3 l = toLocal(Q.m, w);
            l.y = static_cast<Real>(coachOf(train_).floor);
            l.x = std::clamp(l.x, Real(-(coachOf(train_).gangwayHalf - 0.2)), Real(coachOf(train_).gangwayHalf - 0.2));
            car_ = to;
            local_ = l;
            P = Q;
        } else {
            local_.z = std::clamp(local_.z, -hl - half + 0.06, hl + half - 0.06);   // the train's last end: a closed door
            local_.z = std::clamp(local_.z, -hl + 0.2, hl - 0.2);
        }
    }
    // OUT OF THE DOOR: past the body's side where it is open
    if (T.doorSide != 0 && local_.x * T.doorSide > coachOf(train_).width / 2 + 0.3) {
        Vec3 out = P.m.transformPoint(Vec3(T.doorSide * static_cast<Real>(coachOf(train_).width / 2 + 0.5), static_cast<Real>(coachOf(train_).floor), local_.z));
        // the capsule's centre over the platform (its own height, not the 0.7 eye offset: that left a 1.8 m body
        // standing 0.15 m into the platform)
        out.y += (cc ? cc->halfHeight + cc->radius : Real(0.9)) + 0.05;
        leave(out);
        return;
    }

Both ends of a coupling are the same place on the rail, so moving from one car's frame to the next is exact. Out of the door, the capsule is placed over the platform at its own height (the first cut used the eye offset and stood a 1.8 m body 0.15 m into the platform). Debug: trains? over the control channel lists lines, stops and every train; train_board T C puts the player aboard train T, car C.

People take the train

Glenn, watching the first trains: "I don't see anyone on the train though ... there's no sound on the train ... Train stations could use the name of the station." ADR-0156 answered all three.

RailRiders (city_rail_riders.h) pins each station's way in to the nearest walkable street node, in a street network big enough for buses (the bus anchors' rule). A walker's trip into another street network (another town: no street joins them) goes by train when both have a station: startGoalTrip plans it first and aims the street leg (bus if it beats walking) at the station. Within a town, a train is taken only when it saves a minute and a sixth of the walk on trips over ~900 m, the bus's rule (ADR-0157).

    for (std::size_t ti = 0; ti < trains.size(); ++ti) {
        const Train& T = trains[ti];
        if (!T.dwelling || T.doorSide == 0) continue;
        const int stop = T.nextStop;
        const int seats = T.kind.cars * kTrainSeatsPerCar;
        for (int p : riders) {
            RailTrip* t = rail_.tripOf(p);
            if (!t) continue;
            if (t->phase == RailTrip::Phase::Aboard && t->train == static_cast<int>(ti) && t->toStop == stop) {
                t->phase = RailTrip::Phase::Leaving;
                t->timer = rail_.stations()[static_cast<std::size_t>(t->to)].walkIn;
                t->train = -1;
                --aboard[ti];
                ++rail_.stats.setDown;

                       aboard[ti] < seats) {
                t->phase = RailTrip::Phase::Aboard;
                t->train = static_cast<int>(ti);
                ++aboard[ti];
                ++rail_.stats.boarded;
            }
        }

The sim only reads the trains. Seats cap boarding (72 per coach, kTrainSeatsPerCar); the renderer seats each rider in a coach picked by a hash of who they are, in three outfits. Measured headless (ADR-0156): 24 agents sent across two towns all walked in, waited, rode, got off and took the train home, 48 trips. On island 8, in real time, ~50 commuters choose the train at load and walk a median 1.3 km to their station; the first reach a platform about 7 minutes in.

Sounds

All procedural (sfx.h): railClack per axle as it crosses a joint, every 18 m of its own travel, so a car's two bogies give the beat and it quickens with speed; railRumble, a seamless loop on the nearest car scaled by speed; trainHorn, high then low, ~300 m before each platform; trainDoorChime, a falling ding-dong as the doors open and three beeps before they shut. Heard from the camera, so aboard they are simply near.

How it got here

Five days in October. Stage 1 (2026-10-10, ADR-0153) was the test bed: rail_lab.json, a 2.9 km loop with two stations and a bridge each side; lines of 2844 and 2869 m, four trains, ~95 s between stations at up to 22 m/s, every lanes invariant passing. The island loop followed the next day (ADR-0154), then platform access (ADR-0155), riders, sounds and boards (ADR-0156), light rail (ADR-0157/0158) and the station kit (ADR-0159) by 2026-10-12. Most of the work was in the builder, not the trains: three vertical rules had to be learned the hard way.

Rules the builder learned. (1) A shared end is a node, not an overpass: the lift pass skipped nothing at a joint, so two sections of one line "crossed" there and lifted each other 7 m at every joint, 23 km of viaduct. (2) The track moves, not the road: lifting the higher of the two raised Saltwood's streets over the line at 18 %. (3) Holds cross joints: placing joints clear of crossings was not enough in a town; the light rail's first island attempt failed its check with 1.6–4 m steps at joints and a footbridge that came out under a track already 8 m up. That attempt was written, checked and not kept; ADR-0158 fixed both and the city lines went in.

Cost. The island loop added 1.75 M triangles (sleepers 1.14 M, rails 0.44 M, ballast 0.16 M) over 191 cells. Three views at Saltwood with and without the track kit (RT_NO_TRACK_KIT, RT_VSYNC=0): frame p50 17.2/17.3, 18.4/18.2, 13.3/13.0 ms. No measurable cost, so no track LOD yet (ADR-0154).

Not yet

  • Shuttles. An open line's train is put back at its start when it reaches the end (step() says "shuttles owed"); only loops run properly.
  • Level crossings, subways (the subway stock and a metro class name exist in TrainSystem, but no built-in metro track class), tunnels, lifts at stations.
  • Seeing a platform crowd and a full coach on the island in pictures; drawing the walk along the footway (today a timed absence).
