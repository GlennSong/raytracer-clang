Weather and wind

The weather is four states written as data and eased into one another over time. It sets one wind for everything the air moves, and it drives a small fluid simulation of the air over the island and its sea. That simulation decides where clouds form: over sunlit land and windward slopes, carried off downwind, and not over the open sea or over a summit that stands above the inversion.

The problem

A sky has to change: clear days, grey days, a storm now and then, and fronts that arrive rather than cut in. The same weather has to agree everywhere it shows. If the clouds race east while the grass leans west and a parachute drifts north, the world reads as fake. And the clouds have to sit where real clouds sit. Seen from a satellite on a summer afternoon, an island's clouds are organised by the land: cumulus over the sunlit ground and high slopes, clear coasts and sea, clear peaks above the trade-wind cap, and long plumes trailing downwind. Noise scattered evenly over sea and land can't make that pattern.

Three ideas carry the system:

  • A weather is a set of numbers, and the numbers are data. Each state (clear, fair, overcast, storm) is a table in assets/scripts/clouds.lua: cloud cover, light, wind and the air the simulation needs (ADR-0388). Changing weather means easing every number toward the new state's values over about 40 seconds.
  • One wind, one source. DayNightSystem publishes the eased weather as two components, the Wind and the WeatherNow. Everything that moves with the air reads the first, and the simulation follows both (ADR-0337, ADR-0390).
  • Simulate the organisation, not the clouds. A coarse 2D grid of stable fluids (Stam's method) carries the wind, the lift, the humidity and a cloud amount over at least 64 km. The cloud renderer still draws every cloud; the simulation only tells its weather map how much cloud to put where, and of what kind (ADR-0390).

The shape of it

Who owns what. DayNightSystem owns the weather and publishes it; the systems after it (WeatherSimSystem, then CloudMapSystem, in that order in both the editor and the game states) read the components. The wind also reaches the shaders through Renderer::windDir/Speed/Gust. The box at the bottom is where this page hands over to Clouds.

The weather states, as data

A weather state is a point in a space of numbers. The first version (commit 68a1a9b0, 2026-08-17, after Glenn's "most of the time the clouds shouldn't be that dramatic unless there's a storm") made the old default deck the storm state and added a milder fair for everyday skies. The states lived in a C++ switch until ADR-0388 moved them into clouds.lua, under the rule that a new kind of anything is a data edit. Every field is required: a state's numbers are its whole definition, and the loader fails on a missing one rather than guessing.

The fields fall into four groups, and each group has a different reader:

Read the last four fields of each state and you can see how a weather is made rather than painted. Clear has dry air, a low cap and sinking air. Overcast has wet air and a front's lift, so cloud forms over the sea as well as the land, while its weak convection keeps the land from standing out. Storm raises the cap to 9 km, so nothing stops the towers, and has the strongest convection.

Easing from one state to the next

C++ holds the same numbers in one struct, WeatherTargets. Its defaults are fair's, so with no clouds.lua every state is fair rather than a second copy of the table. weatherTargets(kind) reads the loaded cloud book. WeatherNow, the component the simulation reads, is just the eased targets plus an on/off flag:

WeatherTargets' last fields, and WeatherNow src/engine/weather_cycle.h
    // The CLOUD TYPES the weather's map spreads over (ADR-0336; 0 stratus, 1/3 stratocumulus, 2/3 cumulus, 1
    // cumulonimbus): easing between states morphs the types too -- a front's puffs flatten into a deck.
    float typeLo = 0.47f, typeHi = 0.75f;
    // THE WIND the state blows (ADR-0337, wind.h): m/s at 10 m, and how squally
    float windSpeed = 6.0f, windGust = 0.35f;
    // THE SIMULATION's air (ADR-0390, sky/weather_sim.h): the humidity the sea gives it, the inversion's height (m),
    // how strongly sunlit land convects, and the large-scale lift (a front)
    float humidity = 0.80f, inversion = 2000.0f, convection = 1.0f, lift = 0.0f;
};

// THE WEATHER NOW, for the systems that follow it (WeatherSimSystem; rain and sound to come): DayNightSystem publishes
// it each step on the Wind's entity, its targets eased. `active` false: the weather is off (the level's own sky).
struct WeatherNow {
    WeatherTargets live;
    bool active = false;
};

WeatherCycle holds the current state and a live copy of every number. Each fixed step it moves every live value a share of the way to the state's target. The share is 1 − exp(−dt / 40 s), so about 63 % of the gap closes every 40 seconds of simulation time. Because the clouds are placed by these numbers rather than cross-faded between two pictures, a front grows the existing clouds in place. live() packs the live values back into a WeatherTargets, which is what WeatherNow carries.

    // front takes a couple of minutes to fully arrive instead of popping.
    static constexpr double kTauSeconds = 40.0;

    void ease(double dtSeconds) {

        const float a =

        lift += (t.lift - lift) * a;
    }

Two other motions sit beside the easing. snap() jumps straight to the targets, for a state restored at start (yesterday's storm should not fade in from a clear boot) or a now request. The auto walk rolls every 4 in-world hours: half the time the state stays, a quarter of the time it steps one milder, a quarter one harsher. It never skips a neighbour, so a clear noon can't jump-cut to a thunderhead. Since ADR-0340 the walk belongs to a calendar: each in-world day opens with a state drawn from the level's climate (summer and winter odds blended by the day of the year, flipped south of the equator), and the walk within the day is seeded by that day. A given day always has the same weather however you reach it.

The command publishes a WeatherRequest event (ADR-0253's control bus), which DayNightSystem consumes. At startup, three sources compete, in this order:

#SourceRule

2The level's "dayNight": {"weather": ...}off, auto, or a fixed state. A level's "climate" sets the odds, the prevailing wind's bearing and a wind scale.

Remembered weather used to win too often. Every session saved its weather, and the saved state outranked the level and the day's roll, so "the day's roll almost never ran" (ADR-0340). Earlier, ADR-0102, calibrating realism against photographs, had found river_valley coming out overcast (its deck turned the weather to auto, and auto's day was overcast) and noted that "a remembered weather in settings.json beats the level's dayNight.weather by design", so screenshots pinned it with --cmd "weather fair now". ADR-0340 also fixed a seed bug: the level-policy path rolled with a hard-coded 7 where the default path read weather.seed.

Publishing the weather: Wind and WeatherNow

At the end of every fixed step, after easing, DayNightSystem works out the wind and writes both components onto one entity. The direction comes from the climate's prevailing bearing (it blows from that bearing, so the air goes the opposite way; north is −z, east +x) and veers about it with two slow sines: ±0.52 rad over 26 in-world hours plus a smaller 7.3-hour wobble, the "±30°" of ADR-0337. The speed and gustiness are the weather's, or a moderate 6 m/s and 0.35 when the weather is off.

DayNightSystem::fixedUpdate: the wind and the weather now, published src/engine/systems/day_night_system.cpp
    // THE WIND (ADR-0337): the weather's speed and gustiness (a moderate breeze with the weather off); its direction
    // veers slowly through the day about the seed's prevailing one (+-30 degrees over ~26 in-world hours)
    {
        // (the climate's prevailing wind, ADR-0340: it comes FROM that compass bearing, so it goes toward the opposite;
        // north is -z, east +x)
        const double hours = cycle.absoluteDay() * 24.0;
        const double toward = (climate_.prevailingFromDeg + 180.0) * 0.017453292519943295 +
                              0.52 * std::sin(hours * 6.283185307 / 26.0) + 0.17 * std::sin(hours * 6.283185307 / 7.3);
        wind.dir = Vec3(std::sin(toward), 0.0, -std::cos(toward));
        wind.speed = (weatherActive ? weather.windSpeed : 6.0) * climate_.windScale;
        wind.gust = weatherActive ? weather.windGust : 0.35;
        wind.time += ctx.clock.fixedStep();
        if (!ctx.world.alive(windEntity_)) {
            windEntity_ = ctx.world.create();
            ctx.world.add<Wind>(windEntity_, wind);
        } else if (Wind* w = ctx.world.get<Wind>(windEntity_)) {
            *w = wind;
        }
        // ...and the weather now, beside it (ADR-0390: the simulation follows it)
        WeatherNow now;
        now.live = weatherActive ? weather.live() : weatherTargets(WeatherKind::Fair);
        now.active = weatherActive;
        if (WeatherNow* wn = ctx.world.get<WeatherNow>(windEntity_)) *wn = now;
        else ctx.world.add<WeatherNow>(windEntity_, now);
        ctx.renderer.windDir[0] = static_cast<float>(wind.dir.x);
        ctx.renderer.windDir[1] = static_cast<float>(wind.dir.z);
        ctx.renderer.windSpeed = static_cast<float>(wind.speed);
        ctx.renderer.windGust = static_cast<float>(wind.gust);
    }

Easing and publishing use simulation time (ctx.clock.fixedStep()): pausing the game freezes the weather with everything else, and slow motion slows it. The auto walk, though, rides the day's clock, so it holds when the day/night cycle is paused while an in-flight front still finishes arriving.

The wind

Before ADR-0337, "wind" was three unrelated constants: the clouds drifted along +x, grass and trees swayed toward a fixed (0.85, 0.30), and the sea's wave trains had fixed headings. Glenn asked whether the weather's wind could also push "players ... the parachute and wing suit ... grass or trees ... the waves in the sea". The answer was one Wind and one question anyone can ask of it:

wind.h: the Wind, its gusts, and windAt src/engine/wind.h
struct Wind {
    Vec3 dir{0.94, 0.0, 0.34};   // where the air is going, unit, horizontal
    Real speed = 6.0;            // m/s at 10 m above the ground
    Real gust = 0.35;            // 0 steady .. 1 squally: how much stronger a gust is than the mean
    Real time = 0.0;             // seconds: what carries the gust patches downwind
};


// The gust factor at a point: 1 - gust/2 .. 1 + gust, patches ~120 m across travelling with the wind
inline Real windGustFactor(const Wind& w, const Vec3& p) {
    const Real s = 1.0 / 120.0;
    const Vec3 q = p - w.dir * (w.speed * w.time);   // the air that is here now was upwind of it
    const Real n = 0.65 * valueNoise2(q.x * s, q.z * s, 0x57u) + 0.35 * valueNoise2(q.x * s * 2.7 + 5.3, q.z * s * 2.7 - 1.9, 0x57u);
    return 1.0 + w.gust * (1.5 * n - 0.5);
}

// The wind at a point, m/s, horizontal: the mean along `dir`, stronger with height (the power law, a = 0.14, held
// within 0.6 .. 1.8 of the 10 m speed) and gusting
inline Vec3 windAt(const Wind& w, const Vec3& p, Real groundY = 0.0) {
    const Real h = std::max(p.y - groundY, 2.0);
    const Real prof = std::clamp(std::pow(h / 10.0, 0.14), 0.6, 1.8);
    return w.dir * (w.speed * prof * windGustFactor(w, p));
}

Two details are worth reading closely. The gust patches are sampled at p − dir · speed · time: the air over a point now was upwind of it a moment ago, so the noise pattern travels downwind at the wind's speed, and a gust has a place and a direction rather than flickering everywhere at once. The noise is valueNoise2, the engine's one value noise (ADR-0389 found value noise written seven times and kept one). The profile is the meteorologists' power law for wind over open ground, u(h) = u₁₀ (h/10)0.14, clamped so that however high you fly the wind is at most 1.8 times the 10 m wind.

Still one airmass. The Wind component is the same everywhere on the map: trees in the lee of a ridge sway exactly like trees on its crest. Fixed since this page's commit: one gust model, carried by the wind. The shader's sway made its own gust bands from its own clock, while windAt used the noise patches above, so a gust seen crossing a field was not the one a glider met there. And the patches were placed at dir · speed · time, which swung the whole field round the world's origin whenever the wind veered, faster each hour. Since 2026-11-09 (58d12b54, ADR-0391) Wind::time is Wind::drift, the distance the air has actually gone, summed a step at a time (drift += dir · speed · dt), and windGustFactor reads p − drift. mesh.vert reads the very same patches: shaders/vulkan/hash.glsl is hash.h's and noise.h's value noise bit for bit, and the drift rides the globals' wind2.xy. Then (8485c1d8) the sway's ripple, which had rolled along one fixed world axis whatever the wind did, was turned to run downwind, and the bend became a downwind lean that flutters (0.4 to 1.4 of it) instead of a swing that tipped blades into the wind. RT_WIND_TIME still pins the ripple's clock and the clouds' drift; the gust patches follow Wind::drift.

Why simulate the weather

By 2026-11-08 the clouds were placed by a weather map of soft cloud cells (ADR-0386, Clouds), bunched into fields and streets around curl-noise eddies 10–30 km across (ADR-0389). Seen from above with the new cloudmap command it still read wrong. Glenn: "That looks too dense and doesn't look like a real weather system", then: "It feels like there are massive cloud structures that noise can't really create so we should simulate something more realistic."

The reference was real islands. NASA Worldview's MODIS Aqua true-colour views, taken around 13:30 local time on summer afternoons of 2022 and 2023 (CREDITS.md; measured, not shipped), showed the same thing on every island:

Puerto Rico

Popcorn cumulus over the land only, cells 1–3 km, about a third of the land covered; the sea round it about 3 %, with a clear coastal strip. On another date the cells merged into one mass streaming downwind.

Corsica

Cumulus only along the mountain spine; the coast, lowlands and sea clear.

Hawaii

Cloud piled on the windward slopes; the summits, above the trade-wind inversion near 2 km, poke through clear.

Canaries

Small cumulus fields over open sea, and clear wakes in the islands' lee.

The organiser is the land, through physics a noise function can't know about: the sun heats land faster than sea, the heated air rises and draws a sea breeze in from the coast, wind forced up a slope cools and condenses, a cap of warm air (the inversion) stops cumulus at a fixed height, and whatever forms is carried downwind before it evaporates. The weather plan's part C (~/.claude/plans/clouds-weather.md) became ADR-0390: simulate just enough of that to get the pattern.

The simulation

Weather models are 3D and run on supercomputers. This one is deliberately small: a single 2D grid treated as one slab, the boundary layer (the lowest kilometre or two of air, where the ground's heat and roughness are felt). It has no temperature field and no vertical levels. Each physical effect is a term with its own named time or length scale, tuned until the made-up test island looks like the satellite pictures. The diagram shows the effects it keeps, on a cross-section along the wind:

What the grid keeps, drawn in profile (the grid itself is flat: these are its 2D fields read as a story). In the grid, "ground above the inversion" is a wall the wind goes round, not over.

The grid and its fields

WeatherSim::init lays an n×n grid (256 in the engine) over a square height raster and marks each cell land or sea against the level's sea level. WeatherSimSystem makes that raster with rasterizeTerrain, the same function DayNightSystem uses for the mountains' shadow, over the level's square or 64 km, whichever is wider. At 64 km a cell is 250 m. The 64 km minimum exists so a small level still has sea round it for the wind to come from.

Its inputs each step are small: the prevailing wind (direction and speed, from Wind), the sun's height as a sine (sunUp, negative at night), the seconds to advance, and the weather's four numbers from WeatherNow. The class is pure: no engine state, no clock, so the tests drive it directly.

Fixed since this page's commit: the pressure solve converges. Thirty Jacobi sweeps looked fine on average (a mean error of 0.001 in the cover), but measured against a 600-sweep solve on the test island the cover was off by up to 0.29 in places: Jacobi carries a correction one cell a sweep, and the grid is 128 cells wide. Since 2026-11-09 (58d12b54, ADR-0391) the same 30 sweeps are red-black successive over-relaxation (ω = 1.9, kPressureSweeps and kOverRelax), within 0.008 of the converged cover at the same cost. The step's old-wind copy is a member now, not a vector made every sub-step, and the grid starts over when the terrain under it changes (its seed, size or sea level).

Heating, the wind and the sea breeze

The heart of the step is about fifty lines. First the heating: sunlit land heats by the sun's height, from 0.35 at sea level to 1 at 1200 m and above (high ground heats the air more). By night the land cools by up to 0.3 and the sea, warmer than the land, heats by up to 0.15. Everything is scaled by the weather's convection. Then the wind: relaxed toward the prevailing one, carried by itself, and the outer two cells pinned to the prevailing wind, the free air the grid opens onto.

WeatherSim::subStep: the heating, the wind, the projection and the wall src/engine/sky/weather_sim.cpp
void WeatherSim::subStep(const WeatherSimInputs& in, double dt) {
    const WeatherSimWeather& w = in.weather;
    const double U0 = in.windX * in.windSpeed, V0 = in.windZ * in.windSpeed;
    const double day = std::max(0.0, static_cast<double>(in.sunUp));
    const double night = std::clamp(-static_cast<double>(in.sunUp) * 3.0, 0.0, 1.0);
    const double inv = w.inversion;

    // THE HEATING: sunlit land, more on high ground; by night the land cools under the sea's warmth
    double meanHeat = 0.0;
    for (std::size_t k = 0; k < h_.size(); ++k) {
        const double high = std::clamp(h_[k] / 1200.0, 0.0, 1.0);
        const double heat = w.convection * (land_[k] * (day * (0.35 + 0.65 * high) - 0.3 * night) + (1.0 - land_[k]) * 0.15 * night);
        tmp2_[k] = static_cast<float>(heat);
        meanHeat += heat;
    }
    meanHeat /= static_cast<double>(h_.size());

    // THE WIND: back toward the prevailing one; carried by itself; the far edge is the free air
    const double relax = std::min(1.0, dt / kWindRelax);
    // ...
    std::vector<float> uOld = u_;
    advect(u_, uOld, v_, dt, tmp_);
    advect(v_, uOld, v_, dt, tmp_);
    // ... (the outer two cells set to U0, V0)
    // ...made to converge where the heating asks (the projection: grad^2 p = div u - the asked divergence)
    const double inv2c = 1.0 / (2.0 * cell_);
    for (int j = 0; j < n_; ++j)
        for (int i = 0; i < n_; ++i) {
            const int il = std::max(i - 1, 0), ir = std::min(i + 1, n_ - 1), jd = std::max(j - 1, 0), ju = std::min(j + 1, n_ - 1);
            const double d = (u_[at(ir, j)] - u_[at(il, j)]) * inv2c + (v_[at(i, ju)] - v_[at(i, jd)]) * inv2c;
            div_[at(i, j)] = static_cast<float>(d + kBreeze * (tmp2_[at(i, j)] - meanHeat));
        }
    // ... (30 Jacobi sweeps for p)
    for (int j = 0; j < n_; ++j)
        for (int i = 0; i < n_; ++i) {
            // ...
            u_[at(i, j)] -= static_cast<float>((p_[at(ir, j)] - p_[at(il, j)]) * inv2c);
            v_[at(i, j)] -= static_cast<float>((p_[at(i, ju)] - p_[at(i, jd)]) * inv2c);
            // ground standing above the inversion is a wall: the air goes round it
            const double wall = smoothstep(inv - 250.0, inv + 250.0, h_[at(i, j)]);
            u_[at(i, j)] *= static_cast<float>(1.0 - wall);
            v_[at(i, j)] *= static_cast<float>(1.0 - wall);
        }

The projection is the one clever move, borrowed from Jos Stam's "Stable Fluids" (1999). In an ordinary fluid solver the projection removes all divergence: solve ∇²p = ∇·u, subtract ∇p, and the wind neither piles up nor spreads out. Here the source on the right is ∇·u plus kBreeze × (heat − mean heat). Subtracting ∇p then leaves a wind whose divergence is −kBreeze × (heat − mean): it converges over hot land and diverges over the cooler sea, which is what a sea breeze is. Subtracting the mean keeps the total zero, which a pressure solve with closed edges needs. No temperature or buoyancy is simulated; the heating simply asks for convergence and the projection supplies a wind field that delivers it.

The wall follows: where the ground stands within 250 m of the inversion or above it, the wind is faded to nothing, so the air flows round the peak. That is how a Mauna Kea gets a wake on each side and stays clear on top. The advection is semi-Lagrangian: each cell takes what was upwind of it dt ago, read bilinearly through the shared bilinearGrid (since review commit 12695b94; it had its own copy). It is unconditionally stable, which is why Stam's method suits a game: a long step blurs instead of exploding.

Lift, humidity and cloud

WeatherSim::subStep: the lift, the moisture and the cloud src/engine/sky/weather_sim.cpp
    // THE LIFT: the convergence, the wind up the slope (down it: sinking), the heating, the weather's own
    for (int j = 0; j < n_; ++j)
        for (int i = 0; i < n_; ++i) {
            // ...
            const double conv = -d * kConvergenceLift;
            const double slope = (u_[k] * dhx + v_[k] * dhz) * kSlopeLift;
            const double heat = std::max(0.0, static_cast<double>(tmp2_[k])) * kHeatLift;
            // (the outer ring is the free air the grid opens on: pinned to the prevailing wind, its convergence is the
            // pinning's, not the weather's -- it lifted false cloud along the grid's edges)
            const bool ring = i < 4 || j < 4 || i >= n_ - 4 || j >= n_ - 4;
            lift_[k] = static_cast<float>(std::clamp((ring ? 0.0 : conv + slope) + heat + w.lift, -1.0, 2.0));
            conv_[k] = static_cast<float>(std::clamp(heat * 1.4 + std::max(0.0, conv), 0.0, 1.0));
        }

    // THE MOISTURE: carried; the sea moistens the air toward its humidity, the land dries it toward a share of it
    advect(q_, u_, v_, dt, tmp_);
    for (std::size_t k = 0; k < q_.size(); ++k) {
        const double target = land_[k] > 0.5f ? w.humidity * kLandShare : w.humidity;
        const double tau = land_[k] > 0.5f ? kLandDry : kSeaMoisten;
        q_[k] += static_cast<float>((target - q_[k]) * std::min(1.0, dt / tau));
    }

    // THE CLOUD: where the lifted air saturates, and none where the ground stands above the inversion; carried
    for (std::size_t k = 0; k < c_.size(); ++k) {
        const double sat = q_[k] + kLiftMoisture * lift_[k] - kSaturation;
        const double above = smoothstep(inv - 250.0, inv + 250.0, h_[k]);
        const double want = smoothstep(0.0, 0.12, sat) * (1.0 - above);
        const double tau = want > c_[k] ? kCondense : kEvaporate;
        c_[k] += static_cast<float>((want - c_[k]) * std::min(1.0, dt / tau));
    }
    advect(c_, u_, v_, dt, tmp_);

Lift is a sum of four reasons air rises: convergence (air piling in), the wind running up the slope (the dot product of wind and ground gradient; negative in the lee), the heat of a thermal, and the weather's own large-scale lift. The convective share conv_ is kept separately: it says how much of the lift is heat-driven, and the cloud map uses it to lean toward the severe end of the day's cloud types (towering cumulus over hot ground, flatter cloud where a front's lift alone makes it).

The cloud does not have a budget of water. Each cell has a want, from 0 to 1, set by how far humidity plus lift passes saturation, and the cloud relaxes toward it: quickly when growing, slowly when fading. A worked case with the constants: on fair's sea, humidity settles at 0.80, so cloud needs lift of about 0.37 before it starts. On land the air dries toward 0.704 and needs lift near 0.69. The sun alone gives low land 0.6 × 0.35 ≈ 0.21 at noon and high ground about 0.6, so cloud forms only where heat, slope and convergence add up. That is the windward slopes and the heart of the island, not the coast.

Read the four maps against the satellite list. The upwind half of the island has the cloud, where the slope lift and the heating add; the lee half sinks and stays lighter. A plume runs east off the island and fades. The high peak is clear on top, clouded round its flanks, and sheds two streaks from its sides, like Hawaii's. By night the island clears (the land cools, the sea breeze reverses), though the high peak's flanks keep some cloud from the wind forced up them. In the overcast, the front's lift and wet air close the sea, and the island's lee shows a clear wake, the Canaries' pattern.

Sub-steps, and settling

WeatherSim::step and settle src/engine/sky/weather_sim.cpp
void WeatherSim::step(const WeatherSimInputs& in) {
    if (n_ <= 0 || in.dt <= 0.0) return;
    // sub-steps the wind crosses at most a cell in (and no longer than two minutes: the relaxations' resolution)
    const double fastest = std::max(1.0, static_cast<double>(in.windSpeed) * 1.5 + 4.0);
    const double most = std::min(120.0, 0.9 * cell_ / fastest);
    const int steps = std::max(1, static_cast<int>(std::ceil(in.dt / most)));
    for (int s = 0; s < steps; ++s) subStep(in, in.dt / steps);
}

void WeatherSim::settle(const WeatherSimInputs& in, double seconds) {
    WeatherSimInputs s = in;
    s.dt = seconds;
    step(s);
}

Semi-Lagrangian advection is stable at any step, but it is only accurate when the wind moves less than a cell per step, so step splits its time. The "fastest" wind allows half again the prevailing speed plus 4 m/s for the breezes. For fair's 6 m/s on 250 m cells a sub-step is 0.9 × 250 / 13 ≈ 17 s; for a 16 m/s storm about 8 s. The 120 s ceiling only applies on coarse grids. settle is just a long step: four hours by default, which for fair weather is over 800 sub-steps and, per ADR-0390, about 5 s on a worker. Four hours is many times the wind's 40-minute memory and the cloud's 10 and 25 minutes, and longer than the sea's 3-hour moistening, so the pattern has formed; only the land's 8-hour drying is still on its way.

Running it: WeatherSimSystem

The system does three jobs: build the grid once the terrain is known, keep the simulation moving in the weather's time, and hand finished states to the readers without ever blocking a frame.

One simulation, one worker, one immutable snapshot per step. The dashed arrows are reads of the shared snapshot.

WeatherSimSystem::update: the weather's time, the handover, the next step src/engine/systems/weather_sim_system.cpp
void WeatherSimSystem::update(FrameContext& ctx) {
    if (!ctx.view.lighting.volumetricClouds.enabled) return;
    if (!begun_) begin(ctx);
    // the weather's time: the day's clock where it runs, real time where it is held
    const double perSecond = ctx.view.lighting.clockHoursPerSecond > 0.0f ? ctx.view.lighting.clockHoursPerSecond * 3600.0 : 1.0;
    gathered_ += std::clamp(ctx.frameDelta, 0.0, 0.25) * perSecond;
    if (stepping_ && pending_.wait_for(std::chrono::seconds(0)) == std::future_status::ready) {
        Stepped done = pending_.get();
        sim_ = std::move(done.sim);
        snapshot_ = std::move(done.snapshot);
        stepping_ = false;
        publish(ctx);
    }
    if (!stepping_ && sim_.ready() && gathered_ >= kStepEvery) {
        WeatherSimInputs in = inputsNow(ctx);
        in.dt = std::min(gathered_, 3600.0);   // (an hour at most at once: a long stall does not leap the sky)
        gathered_ = 0.0;
        pending_ = std::async(std::launch::async, [sim = std::move(sim_), in]() mutable {
            sim.step(in);
            std::shared_ptr<const WeatherSim> snapshot = std::make_shared<const WeatherSim>(sim);
            return Stepped{std::move(sim), std::move(snapshot)};
        });
        stepping_ = true;
    }
}
  • The weather's time. The simulation advances on the day's clock: on a 30-minute day, an afternoon's convection builds and decays in minutes of play, in step with the sun. When the clock is held, it runs at real time so the sky still lives. Frame time is clamped to 0.25 s, so a hitch does not count as weather.
  • Batched steps. Weather seconds pile up in gathered_ and are stepped in one go once 30 have gathered and the worker is free. A long stall is capped at an hour of weather at once.
  • The sun's height comes from the clock alone, sin((hours − 6) · π / 12): 0 at 06:00 and 18:00, 1 at noon. The comment in inputsNow says the latitude's finer arc is the sky's business, not the weather's. "Night" in the heating saturates once that sine is below −⅓, about 19:20 to 04:40.
  • The snapshot. The WeatherSim itself is moved into the worker's lambda and back, so only one thread ever touches it. Each step also makes an immutable copy, shared_ptr<const WeatherSim>, and that copy is what is published. Readers (the cloud map bake, on another worker) keep their copy alive as long as they need it, while the next step runs on the original.
WeatherField: what the simulation publishes src/engine/sky/weather_sim.h
// THE WEATHER FIELD the simulation last finished (WeatherSimSystem publishes it on an entity of its own): read-only,
// shared, swapped whole -- the weather map bakes from it on a worker while the next step runs
struct WeatherField {
    std::shared_ptr<const WeatherSim> sim;
    uint64_t version = 0;   // counts the handovers
};

Who copies the snapshot. In the first version (commit 28155245) the worker returned the stepped simulation, and publish() copied it into a new shared_ptr on the main thread: twelve 256² float arrays, about 3 MB per handover, inside a frame. The review fixes for #198 (commit 12695b94, "The weather's snapshot is copied on the worker, not the main thread") made the worker return a Stepped pair, the simulation and its finished snapshot, so the main thread only moves pointers. The same commit replaced the simulation's two hand-written bilinear reads with the shared bilinearGrid, and its hand-written π with PI.

begin() runs on the first frame that has a volumetric cloud deck. It reads the level's TerrainLodConfig, rasterizes the terrain over the larger of the level and 64 km, and logs the grid ("[weather] simulation over 64 km, 256^2 cells of 250 m, with its sea"). A level with no terrain gets flat land and no sea. The first step is settle() on the worker, so the first sky the player sees has already formed rather than growing from nothing.

How it feeds the clouds

The simulation produces no cloud shapes. The clouds are drawn by the volumetric march from a weather map: an 80 km square of the sky round the camera, baked on a worker by CloudMapSystem, in which every cloud is a soft footprint of some type and height (Clouds explains all of that). With a WeatherField present, the map takes three things from it:

bakeCloudMap: each grid square's cells from the simulated cover and convection src/engine/sky/cloud_map.cpp
                if (in.field) {
                    // the simulation's cloud here, as footprints' worth per m^2, shared by the types its convection leans
                    // to (this type's share of a narrow range round that point on the axis)
                    const double wx = ax - in.driftX, wz = az - in.driftZ;
                    const double c = std::min(0.97, static_cast<double>(in.field->coverAt(wx, wz)));
                    const double t = in.typeLo + (in.typeHi - in.typeLo) * in.field->convectionAt(wx, wz);
                    const std::array<float, 4> share = cloudTypeWeights(static_cast<float>(t - 0.08), static_cast<float>(t + 0.08));
                    m = -std::log(1.0 - c) * share[static_cast<std::size_t>(k)] * g.spacing * g.spacing / meanAreaOf[k];
  1. Cover → how many cells (λ). Cells dropped at random with density λ per m² leave a share exp(−λ·A) of the sky uncovered, where A is a cell's area. So to cover a share c, the bake asks for λ·A = −ln(1 − c) footprints' worth per square. Cover is capped at 0.97 so the logarithm stays finite. The map is laid out in the moving air (the world plus the clouds' drift), so it reads the simulation at the ground point under the air, air − drift.
  2. Convection → which type. The convective share picks a point on the day's type range (fair: 0.47–0.75), and the square's cells go to the types within ±0.08 of it: hot ground gets cumulus, a front's lift the flatter end.
  3. Closed sky → one mass. Where the simulated cover passes 0.55, a solid footprint fades in (full at 0.92), typed by the convection and at full height: "the structures no scatter of cells makes (a merged afternoon mass over a mountain, a front's deck)".

                    const double wx = x - in.driftX, wz = z - in.driftZ;

                        best = mass;

                        height = 1.0f;
                    }
                }

With a field present, the weather's coverage knob and ADR-0389's eddy-swirled moisture field stand aside: the simulation says where. A new field version triggers a rebake at most every 10 seconds (ADR-0390 measured 0.7 s per bake). The weather map in turn drives the clouds' shadows on the ground (ADR-0387), so the simulation also decides where the island is in shade.

The fair-afternoon test: the land, not the sea tests/test_weather_sim.cpp
TEST_CASE(weather_sim_fair_afternoon_clouds_the_land_not_the_sea) {
    WeatherSim s;
    s.init(madeWorld(), 0.0, 256);
    s.settle(fairAfternoon());
    const float land = meanCover(s, [](double x, double z) { return std::hypot(x, z) < 10000.0; });

Known gaps

Too much cloud on land (#199)

A fair afternoon covers ~0.53 of the test island against Puerto Rico's ~0.35. ADR-0390's first owed item; issue #199.

Local wind for foliage (#200)

The simulation's wind varies by place, but trees, grass and particles still read the one Wind. Issue #200.

Rain (#201)

Precipitation from deep wet cells: rain shafts, particles, wet surfaces, sound (the plan's part D). Issue #201.

Fronts still walk

The day's state is still drawn from the calendar and walked; ADR-0340 expected the simulation to bring fronts in from the prevailing wind instead. Metal has no simulation-driven clouds yet (ADR-0390, owed).
