How Buildings Are Grown

No building in the city is a modeled asset. Each one is grown by a shape grammar: a lot polygon and a recipe go in, and a real structure — floors, windows, doors, cornices, a fitted roof — comes out, sized to the plot it stands on. This is the same split-grammar idea behind procedural cities in film and games (ADR-0038), rebuilt as a small, composable C++ vocabulary.

Recipe → parameters → grammar

The architect (see how the city is built) picks a recipe for each lot — glass_tower, rowhouses, courthouse — which is really a named bundle of BuildingParams. Those parameters drive the grammar. A few of the ~30 knobs:

floors floorHeight 3.2m groundRetail bayWidth 3.5m curtainWall quoins pilasters stringCourse cornice portico dome groundBays setbackEvery roof pitch

Everything is dimensioned in real metres from human constants — a 2.7 m door reads as an entrance, a 4.5 m ground floor reads as retail, windows sit on a 0.9 m sill — so the output is legibly at human scale rather than an abstract mass.

1 · The floorplan

A building takes the shape of its lot, not a bounding box. The simplified lot polygon is inset until every corner clears the road corridor, then becomes the footprint that's extruded upward. That single choice is what lets L-shaped, triangular, and flatiron plots become interesting buildings instead of forced rectangles:

Courtyard carves

A big mid-rise cuts a court into its back side, so the mass reads as an L, U, or T with wings — away from the street, so road clearance still holds.

Rounded prows

A very acute corner would grow a knife-edge facade, so it's rounded into a short chord arc — the classic flatiron nose.

Houses on yards

A house sits on a small centred rectangle, not the whole lot — the rest reads as its garden.

Terraces

A rowhouse lot becomes a run of narrow townhome units sharing party walls, each with its own door and stoop, grown side by side with zero gaps.

A plan-quality gate rejects genuinely degenerate slivers (they become green space instead), and a tall building on a pinched plan is shortened to what its floor plate can actually carry — no skyscraper on a knife of a lot.

2 · Tripartite composition

Height drives structure and cladding (ADR-0040): a building is composed as base → shaft → cap, like real architecture.

The base is a taller ground floor with a real entrance (the wall is hollowed and a door punched through, so the building reads as enterable), optional retail glazing, and a string-course band on top. The shaft repeats the residential floors with the facade grammar below. The cap is a fitted roof. Tall towers step their mass back every few floors (setbackEvery) for a tapered silhouette.

3 · The facade grammar

Each wall is divided into bays — window modules about 3.5 m wide — and every bay is filled by an opening whose real shape is cut into the wall. Openings are not decals; the hole is genuine geometry:

  • Windows carry a sill, a recessed frame (windowInset), and muntins that partition the glass into panes (lightsX × lightsY). A head can be square, a segmental arch (with a rise fraction), or capped by a projecting hood — a band or an arched drip-mould.
  • Doors are a double-leaf 2.0 m × 2.7 m entrance on the street face, aimed at the nearest road, optionally under a projecting awning and reached by entrance steps on sloped ground.
  • Quoins stack dressed corner stones up the building edges; pilasters run vertical piers framing each bay full height; a cornice oversails the top. These are the details that separate a civic hall from an apartment block.
  • Curtain walls flip the whole facade to structural glass with mullions — the downtown towers — with special corner mullions so the glass wraps cleanly instead of leaving a gap.
  • Ground bays replace the entrance with a run of slatted roller doors — the industrial/fire-station front.

4 · Roofs

The cap is chosen to fit the building. Flat commercial roofs get a parapet railing with an optional lip and a roof plant subgrammar — a bulkhead with a door, HVAC louvres, and fans — so the top reads as mechanical plant rather than a bare grey block. Houses get pitched roofs with proper soffits (no see-through undersides). Civic landmarks can carry a crown or a lathe-turned dome and cupola. The roof always accounts for its own rise in the building height, so nothing pokes through.

5 · The mesh-op library

The fancier elements are built from a small library of reusable mesh operations — the same primitives you'd find in a modeling package, as code (also exposed to Lua as mesh.lathe). A columned civic portico is not a bespoke mesh; it is steps + an array of lathed columns + an entablature + a pediment:

OpBuilds
latheRevolve a 2-D profile around an axis — the core of every turned form.
columnA classical column: plinth, base rings, entasis shaft, capital, abacus.
entranceStepsA porch platform with descending steps to the door.
porticoSteps + a colonnade (a linear array of columns) + entablature + triangular pediment.
rotundaDrum + a radial array of columns + entablature ring + dome + cupola — the capitol crown.

See the procedural toolkit for how the mesh-op library and the rest of the engine's generators (L-systems, noise, the material library) fit together.

6 · Materials

Every grammar element is tagged with a part class — Wall, Glass, Trim, Roof, Door, Brick, Concrete, Stucco, Metal, Wood — and the whole district is merged into one mesh per class. Each class binds an analytic PBR surface from the material library: procedural brick with staggered courses and recessed mortar, scored concrete, warm stone, glass. So a thousand buildings draw as a handful of textured meshes, all lit as real physically-based materials rather than flat colour.

7 · The style book — data, not code

The C++ architect decides what stands where (deterministic, unit-tested). A separate Lua style book (assets/scripts/style_book.lua) owns how it looks: per-recipe overrides for cladding, window pattern, colours, trim (ADR-0042). It's pure data, hot-reloadable — edit the file and the city restyles without a rebuild — so the look can be tuned without touching the placement logic.
